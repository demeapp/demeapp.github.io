About & method

How Deme works

What Deme is

Deme is a non-partisan project that makes Toronto City Council's voting record readable. Council publishes its decisions and votes as official records. Deme reorganizes the same facts into plain language. It keeps them searchable — by topic, by ward, and by the person who cast the vote.

Sources

Everything here comes from the City of Toronto's published council records. Those are the agenda items, decisions, and voting records the City itself posts. They are the same records linked from every decision page as the “official record.” Deme adds no outside facts about any member or decision.

How the briefs are written

Each decision carries a short brief. It is a plain-language summary of what the item is and what was decided. Each brief is prepared from the official record. Each is written to be read in under a minute. Briefs summarize — they never replace the record. The official wording is one tap away on every decision page. Where a brief and the official record ever differ, the official record is right.

The line that says why it matters

Many decisions carry one extra line under the brief. The line says, in plain words, why the decision matters. Each line is composed by template from the record's own fields. It draws on the topic, the outcome, and the recorded-vote result where one exists. It also uses amounts named in the official summary. If a line can't be sourced from the record, it isn't written: some decisions simply have no line.

Story threads

Decisions are also grouped into story threads by documented keyword rules over their titles and subject terms. The grouping is conservative — when in doubt a decision is left out — and a decision can appear in more than one thread. Threads are a finding aid: they change nothing in the record, and every decision in a thread is the record's own, brief and outcome included.

Asking in plain words

Search understands plain questions — topics, names, years, outcomes. It shows its working in a line above the results. You can see exactly what it took your words to mean. You can change any of it with the filters. It only ever retrieves real decisions; if it can't break a question down, it says so and runs a plain search instead.

Neutrality

Deme takes no side. It publishes no grades, no rankings, no endorsements, and no characterizations of any member. Every member's voting record is shown in the same format, with the same fields: attendance as a plain statistic, votes cast, declared interests, and the item-by-item record. Readers draw their own conclusions.

One rule deserves spelling out. When a member declares a personal interest in an item, stepping back from that vote is a duty, not an absence. Deme never counts declared interests against a member's attendance.

Weighing in, and your privacy

Before a Council vote, you can weigh in — Support or Oppose — on items in the published agenda. Weighing in takes a free account: your pick is saved to your account, where only you can see it, so your voting card and notifications can follow you on any device. Deme keeps no tally of visitors' positions. It does not know how many people picked either side. It never presents visitors' picks as a result. The only results shown are Council's. Your profile — your ward, a watched ward, and your topics — lives in your browser, and on your account when you create one. With an account, your name, email, wards, topics and picks are held on our server, so they can follow you across devices. If you sign up for Council alerts, your email is held on your account so digests can reach you. The privacy policy lists exactly what is held, where, and how to delete it.

Coverage — and its limits

The decision archive covers 2018 to 2026 and is current to October 19, 2026. The voting record reaches back further — five terms, to 2006. That is why some long-serving members' totals include votes from before the decision archive begins. Their item-by-item record starts in 2018. About 51% of decisions are tagged to a specific ward. The rest are citywide decisions — they apply across Toronto — or carry no ward in the record.

Only about 9 in every 100 decisions carries a recorded (name-by-name) vote. For the rest, the public record attaches no member's position. Deme says so rather than guessing. Where a “close vote” is called — in Explore's close-votes view, and in the vote counts printed on the decision tiles — the margin is the margin of the vote that decided the item: its final adoption vote at City Council. The closest-calls dataset behind the member-alignment figures measures an item differently on purpose: its margin comes from the item's first City Council-stage recorded vote — its main vote. On an item with several recorded votes, the two margins can differ. The most recent Council meeting in this archive is July 29, 2026. The weigh-in list in My Record shows only City Council items from published agendas — never committee or board business — and only while they are still upcoming.

Questions, answered plainly

Is Deme free?

Yes — completely. Every decision, brief and tool here is free for everyone. There is nothing to buy and no premium tier.

Is Deme partisan?

No. Deme takes no side: it publishes no grades, no rankings and no endorsements, and every member is shown in the same format. Each brief is a plain-language summary of the official record — and the record itself is one tap away on every decision page, so you can check the wording against the source.

What exactly do I get?

The published record in plain language: 33,796 decisions, every recorded vote by name, your own councillor's full history, and the promises they campaigned on. Set your ward and Deme shows your area first. When the new Council term begins on November 15, 2026, email digests of upcoming Council business begin for anyone who signs up for Council alerts.

What do you collect about me?

As little as possible. If you weigh in, your picks are saved to your account — the item, your position, and when you took it — where only you can read or change them; they are used only for your own voting card and notifications, and Deme keeps no tally of them. Your ward and topics live in your browser, and on your account if you create one. If you create an account, your name, email, wards and topics are held on our server so they can follow you across devices, and you can export or delete everything at any time. The privacy policy lists exactly what is held, where, and how to delete it.

Which governments does Deme cover?

Toronto City Council — the decision archive from 2018 to 2026 and the voting record across five terms. Deme is built so other councils can be added the same way; Toronto is the record published today.

When does Deme launch?

It's live — the site you're reading is the launch, and the record is current to October 19, 2026. Toronto votes on October 26, 2026, and the new Council term begins November 15, 2026. The record keeps growing with every meeting.

The record as open data

Every data file this site loads is published at a public address. The open data page lists each file, what it holds, where the record comes from, and how anyone can reuse it — no account, no key; the City's data is shared under the City's open licence.
