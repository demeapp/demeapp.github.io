Councillor Fletcher asks staff to explore a food hub for east Toronto.

Official title: Food Hub for East End

AdoptedJan 17, 2018 · Toronto City Council
No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

When the Red Door Shelter moved to Gerrard Street East, the Community Development and Recreation Committee supported the move by ensuring adequate community supports would be available, including using the nearby Glen Rhodes Church which has an active food bank. Since the move I have come to understand that the food bank which operates out of Glen Rhodes is an extremely important community resource, used by families from all over the east end of Toronto. In these times of increased rent and expenses, the food bank is even more important in order to keep people well fed. The exceptional volunteers also run a weekly meal which also creates community for low income residents. The food bank has widespread support from businesses across Gerrard Street. During our discussions about support for the Red Door with the church, the issue a potential food hub was raised. This is an exciting idea…

What was decided

The Community Development and Recreation Committee: 1. Requested the Deputy City Manager, Cluster A to assist in the exploration of increasing food access in east Toronto through the potential of a food hub at Glen Rhodes United Church or another suitable location to meet the deep needs of the community through establishing a working group in consultation with the local Councillor.
