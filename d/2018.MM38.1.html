What the item proposed

In 2018, the Prince Edward Viaduct will celebrate the centennial of its opening, bridging the Don River and providing access to Torontonians between Bloor Street and Danforth Avenue. Designed by architect Edmund Burke with engineer Thomas Taylor, the bridge took four years to complete. It was a significant public works project that employed hundreds, many of whom were recent immigrants. The builders demonstrated foresight by including a lower deck to accommodate a subway, even though Toronto would not have an east-west subway until 1966. Prior to the Viaduct's completion, the most northerly crossing of the Don River was at Winchester Street, near Riverdale Park. The access afforded by the new bridge spurred development east of the Don, unifying the City, and stimulating economic growth.
