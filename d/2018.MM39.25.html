The official record

What the item proposed

There are now more seniors living in Toronto than children. The growth in the seniors population is expected to accelerate in coming years. By 2031, one quarter of the City's population will be over the age of 60. The City recognizes that there are policy, service and resource implications to this significant demographic shift. At the same time, the increased life expectancy is a triumph and we all benefit from the energy, resilience and contribution of Toronto seniors. In 2013, City Council approved the Toronto Seniors Strategy Version 1.0. As a result of Version 1.0 and the progress of implementation, in 2016 Toronto was recognized by the World Health Organization as a Global Age-Friendly City. On March 26, 2018, the City of Toronto was recognized as a leader in creating inclusive and accessible environments for seniors with the first ever Ontario Age-Friendly Community Recognition…

What was decided

City Council on April 24, 25, 26 and 27, 2018, adopted the following: 1. City Council authorize the Executive Director, Social Development, Finance and Administration to enter into an agreement with the Ministry of Seniors Affairs to receive $44,880 in grant funding to deliver a pilot HomeShare program for seniors to deliver the initiative. 2. City Council increase the 2018 Approved Operating Budget for Social Development, Finance and Administration by $44,880 gross, $0 net, with funding provided by the Ministry of Seniors Affairs.
