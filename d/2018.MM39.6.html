The official record

What the item proposed

The last few years have seen a dramatic rise in rat control related complaints by residents and business owners residing along main streets in the west-end of Toronto. The urban landscape creates an environment for rats to thrive by finding an ample supply of food, water and shelter. Rats have the capacity to burrow and live in common spaces such as planters, pipes, sewers, garbage bins, unmaintained buildings, basements and prefer to live in generally wet and low environments. The capacity of rats to reproduce and adapt creates challenges in finding appropriate mitigation strategies. Without proper counter measures, rats will continue to reproduce and survive as it is becoming easier for them to travel and find shelter in harsh winters. What was decided

City Council on April 24, 25, 26 and 27, 2018, adopted the following: 1. City Council request the City Manager, the Medical Officer of Health, and the Executive Director, Municipal Licensing and Standards to report to the Licensing and Standards Committee in 2019 on: a. possible improvements to mitigation strategies and rat control education initiatives to assist residents and businesses; and b. current work being undertaken by the City and its agencies and divisions to incorporate preventative rat mitigation strategies into capital works projects.
