Affirming Support for a Minimum Wage in Toronto - by Councillor Neethan Shan, seconded by Councillor Maria Augimeri

AdoptedJun 26, 2018 · Toronto City Council

City Council was asked to affirm its support for the Minimum Wage increase to $15 an hour starting January 1, 2019 and encourage the Ontario Government to not rescind the law increasing the minimum wage.

The official summary names a figure of $14. Decided by a recorded vote, 22 in favour and 7 against — every member's position is public.

The official record

What the item proposed

Last year the Ontario Government passed a bill raising the minimum wage to $14 an hour for January 1, 2018 and $15 an hour for January 1, 2019. This increase was supported by many groups across Toronto, from faith groups, labour organizations, student groups and many other stakeholders. A living wage is something the people in Toronto need to prevent us from having a large number of residents be working poor. Many people who work full time still struggle to pay for housing, food and other essentials. Women, newcomers and racialized communities are over-represented among minimum wage earners. Raising the minimum wage will help close the income gap for workers facing barriers and income inequalities. What was decided

City Council on June 26, 27, 28 and 29, 2018, adopted the following: 1. City Council affirm its support for the Minimum Wage increase to $15 an hour starting January 1, 2019 and encourage the Ontario Government to not rescind the law increasing the minimum wage.
