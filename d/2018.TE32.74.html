Speed Hump Poll Results - St. Joseph Street

Adopted with changesMay 2, 2018 · Toronto City Council

Toronto and East York Community Council was asked to deny the installation of speed humps on St. Joseph Street, between Queen's Park Crescent East and Bay Street.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

This staff report is a matter that Community Council has delegated authority to make a final decision, provided that it is not amended so that it varies with City policy or by-laws. Transportation Services is reporting on the results of the traffic calming poll undertaken on St. Joseph Street, between Queen's Park Crescent East and Bay Street. The poll provided a response rate of less than the 50 percent plus one ballot required by the traffic calming policy in order to confirm that there is a favourable consensus in the community to install speed humps on the subject section of St. Joseph Street. Accordingly, continuing the process to install speed humps on St. Joseph Street, between Queen's Park Crescent East and Bay Street, is not recommended.

What was decided

The Toronto and East York Community Council: 1. Directed the City Solicitor to prepare a by-law to alter the roadway to install three speed humps on St. Joseph Street, between Queen's Park Crescent East and Bay Street, generally as shown on Drawing No. 421G-2800, dated December 2017, attached to the report (March 22, 2018) from the Acting Director, Transportation Services, Toronto and East York District. 2. Approved the reduction of the speed limit from 40 km/h to 30 km/h on St. Joseph Street, between Queen's Park Crescent East and Bay Street, when speed humps are installed.
