Speed Hump Installation on Sammon Avenue between Coxwell Avenue and Woodbine Avenue

Adopted with changesJul 4, 2018 · Toronto City Council

The Toronto and East York Community Council was asked to should direct the City Solicitor to prepare a by-law to alter the roadway to install eleven speed humps on Sammon Avenue, between Coxwell Avenue and Woodbine Avenue, for traffic calming purposes.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

In 2017 a traffic volume and speed study was conducted to determine whether traffic calming was warranted on Sammon Avenue between Coxwell and Woodbine Avenues. The study demonstrated that the 85th percentage ranged between 39 and 47 km/hr. with traffic volumes that exceeded or almost met the warrants on all blocks. https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2017/te/bgrd/backgroundfile-105986.pdf TEYCC approved my motion to conduct a poll in September 2017. The poll narrowly missed the minimum response rate, but of the ballots cast, 68% of the residents were in favour of speed humps. However, a request to proceed with traffic calming did not come forward to Community Council. There has been a renewed interest in the installation of speed humps along Sammon Avenue. What was decided

The Toronto and Eats York Community Council: 1. Directed the City Solicitor to prepare a by-law to alter the roadway to install eleven speed humps on Sammon Avenue, between Coxwell Avenue and Woodbine Avenue, for traffic calming purposes, generally as shown on Drawing No. 421G-2547, dated July, 2017. 2. Authorized the Director of Transportation Services for Toronto and East York District to determine the timing for the installation of speed humps on Sammon between Coxwell Avenue and Woodington Avenue in consultation with the Ward Councillor.
