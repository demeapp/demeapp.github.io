What the item proposed

Consistent monitoring of food affordability and food insecurity is necessary to assess the health of the population, to evaluate policy change, and to develop evidence-based recommendations for public health interventions. Based on this year's assessment, Toronto Public Health is reporting that the cost of nutritious food in Toronto has risen 7.6 percent between 2018 and 2019, the largest year-over-year increase in a decade. Ongoing surveillance will determine if there is evidence of a trend and what changes, if any, should be made to current programs and policies, particularly those targeting food insecurity. Household food insecurity is defined as inadequate access to food due to financial constraints. What was decided

City Council on November 26 and 27, 2019, adopted the following: 1. City Council request the Government of Canada to: a. update the list of foods included in the National Nutritious Food Basket to reflect the recommendations from the 2019 Canada's Food Guide; b. develop a national food costing protocol to standardize the annual monitoring of food affordability by provincial/territorial and local governments; and c. mandate the annual measurement of food insecurity using the Household Food Security Survey Module.
