Councillor Fletcher asks for a study of all-way stops on Alton Avenue.

Official title: Improve Pedestrian Safety on Alton Avenue

AdoptedJan 15, 2019 · Toronto City Council
No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

Alton Avenue is a one-way northbound street which runs north-south adjacent to Greenwood Park. As such, Alton Avenue is used heavily year round by pedestrians accessing the park, renovated skating rink or outdoor pool. Where Alton Avenue intersects with Queen Street East, Dundas Street East and Gerrard Street East, there are pedestrian crosswalks which allow people to cross these major intersecting roads safely. There are no crosswalks or traffic control signals which cross Alton Avenue from the east side to the west side. Due to the fact that Alton Avenue is adjacent to Greenwood Park, pedestrians are crossing at unmarked, uncontrolled locations on a daily basis putting their safety at risk. This is exacerbated by the fact that there are no stop signs, stop bars or corner curb cuts where Alton Avenue intersects with Stanton Avenue or Sawden Avenue. This motion includes recommendations…

What was decided

The Toronto and East York Community Council: 1. Directed Transportation Services, to study and report back on the feasibility of installing all way stop controls on Alton Avenue at Stanton Avenue, and Alton Avenue and Sawden Avenue to Toronto and East York Community Council at its meeting on May 22, 2019. 2. Directed Transportation Services, to study and report back on the feasibility of installing curb cuts to facilitate pedestrian crossings at Alton Avenue and Sawden Avenue, and Parkfield Avenue and Stanton Avenue to Toronto and East York Community Council at its meeting on May 22, 2019.
