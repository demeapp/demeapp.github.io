City Council was asked to use ward names instead of numbers on public documents.

Official title: Use of Ward Names to identify City Wards and Build Better Community Connections - by Councillor Anthony Perruzza, seconded by Councillor James Pasternak

AdoptedSep 30, 2020 · Toronto City Council
No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

Historically, it has been the City's practice to use mainly numbers to identify City Wards. This made sense when the Council was composed of Wards that shared the same name. A Ward number was a necessary unique identifier. Now, as required by O.Reg. 408/18, the City's 25 Wards each have a unique name that denotes the geographic area that Ward represents. However, the practice of using Ward numbers has continued. A geographically based Ward identifier is more easily understood than a Ward number as it points to a geographical area. Residents of the City can better identify the area in which they live by name rather than number. A ward name also allows the City to build better connections to the communities they serve. What was decided

City Council on September 30, October 1 and 2, 2020, adopted the following: 1. City Council direct the City Manager to require City staff, where possible, to use Ward names only on all public facing documentation and identifiers.
