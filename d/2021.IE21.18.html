The official record

What the item proposed

E-commerce and app-based food and goods delivery is on the rise in Toronto. It has become essential in the pandemic to allow people to stay home and stay safe, but it is clear that this is also a greater long term shift in our economy. Delivery couriers are considered essential workers, but they face significant challenges and often struggle to make a living wage because they are misclassified as independent contractors and are paid per delivery. Being able to find car and bike parking easily and avoiding being ticketed is key to being able to survive financially since completing their job within a timely manner impacts the revenue they obtain. With the increasing number of deliveries, couriers are struggling to find legal parking and are being ticketed at an increasing rate. What was decided

The Infrastructure and Environment Committee: 1. Requested the General Manager, Transportation Services to explore the possibility of offering a special permit that allows delivery vehicles to park temporarily in areas that are designated as a ‘no parking zone’, and in consultation with organizations that represent delivery workers and report back in the fourth quarter of 2021 to the Infrastructure and Environment Committee. 2. Requested the General Manager, Transportation Services to develop and communicate a plan to increase bicycle parking in high-delivery areas in Toronto.
