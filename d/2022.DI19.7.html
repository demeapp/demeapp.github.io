The committee asks for an update on enforcing the e-scooter ban.

Official title: Enforcing the Prohibition of E-Scooters in the Public Realm

AdoptedFeb 22, 2022 · Toronto City Council
No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

At the May 5, 2021 meeting of City Council, a recommendation to decline the option to participate in O.Reg 389/19 - Pilot Project - Electric Kick-Scooters was adopted Agenda Item History - 2021.IE21.7 At that time, City Council requested that City staff work with the Toronto Police Service Board to develop a public education campaign to convey the existing by-laws as well as actively scale up city-wide enforcement on the prohibition of e-scooters. Since that time, there have been reports about the continued and expanding use of e-scooters through the city, using the prohibited public sidewalk, park pathways, and other pedestrian-oriented space. E-scooters are virtually silent and pose a threat to anyone who is blind or has mobility limitations navigating the sidewalks. Additionally, any e-scooters randomly abandoned in the public right-of-way as witnessed in other cities create barriers…

What was decided

The Toronto Accessibility Advisory Committee recommends that: 1. The City Manager, in consultation with relevant City divisions, provide an update to the Toronto Accessibility Advisory Committee at its May 5, 2022 meeting, on the progress of the public education campaign for the prohibition of e-scooters in the public realm, the plan on city-wide enforcement and confirmation of the necessary funding for 2022 and future budget processes.
