Toronto City Council · Decision

Councillor Minnan-Wong asks for biennial reports on Toronto traffic congestion, starting in mid-2023.

Official title: Monitoring Traffic Congestion

AdoptedJul 7, 2022 · Toronto City Council
No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

Now that the COVID-19 pandemic is receding in the rear view mirror, people are returning to their regular travel patterns. Along with this we are seeing an increase in traffic congestion. While some traffic congestion is to be expected, many residents are experiencing much longer travel times compared to pre-COVID conditions, which will likely worsen as more people return to the workplace. INRIX, a company that collects and analyses global data on traffic and congestion, found that in 2021 Toronto was the most congested city in Canada, the 22nd worst congested city in the world, and drivers lost 74 hours in traffic. Traffic congestion patterns and trends must be monitored to fully understand changes in residents' ability to move about the city.

What was decided

The Infrastructure and Environment Committee: 1. Requested the General Manager, Transportation Services, to report every two years on traffic congestion levels in Toronto, starting in the Second Quarter of 2023, and requested the General Manager, Transportation Services, to include data on time spent in traffic for major routes within the City, with comparative data for previous years.
