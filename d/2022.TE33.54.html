Toronto City Council · Decision

Community Council was asked to add an on-street accessible parking space on Earlscourt Avenue for residents with mobility issues.

Official title: Accessible Parking Space Installation - 127 Earlscourt Avenue

AdoptedMay 26, 2022 · Toronto City Council
No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

Recently, I have heard from the homeowner of 127 Earlscourt Avenue requesting the installation of an accessible parking space (APS). In the reported case, both residents are seniors with severe mobility and health issues. For these reasons they have applied and qualified for an accessible parking permit. Traffic Operations staff conducted an investigation of the property and noted that an on-street accessible space would not be supported under their guidelines. However, by way of Toronto East York Community Council I am making an amendment to facilitate the installation of an accessible parking space in order to provide the residents with convenience and an improved quality of life.

What was decided

The Toronto and East York Community Council: 1. Designated an on-street accessible parking space on the west side of Earlscourt Avenue, between a point 73.4 metres south of Hope Street and a point 5.5 metres further south inclusive, from the 16th day to the last day of each month, Apr. 1st to Nov. 30th, inclusive. 2. Designated an on-street accessible parking space on the east side of Earlscourt Avenue, between a point 76 metres south of Hope Street and a point 5.5 metres further south inclusive, from the 1st day of Dec. to the 31st day of Mar. of the next following year, inclusive and from the 1st day to the 15th day of each month Apr. 1st to Nov. 30th, inclusive.
