Audit of Transportation Services: Improving Utility Cut Permit and Inspection Processes

AdoptedJul 23, 2025 · Toronto City Council

The Auditor General checked the permit process for utility digging to reach underground pipes and cables, and wants faster, more consistent reviews. Council is asked to direct Transportation Services to act.

The official record

What the item proposed

The Transportation Services Division maintains City roads and sidewalks to ensure they remain safe and accessible for all users. Each year, the Utility Management Unit of Transportation Services issues approximately 35,000 utility cut permits to authorized public utility companies. The Auditor General’s 2025 Work Plan included an audit of Transportation Services’ Utility Cut Program to review its permit and inspection processes. A “utility cut” refers to excavating a portion of the public right-of-way (e.g., pavement, sidewalks or boulevards) to provide access to underground utilities, such as water mains, power lines, and telecommunications infrastructure. Utility cuts can lead to traffic disruptions and construction-related dust and noise complaints. What was decided

City Council on July 23 and 24, 2025, adopted the following: 1. City Council request the General Manager, Transportation Services, to improve tracking and timeliness of the utility cut permit application review and approval process by: a. ensuring that permit status and key application timelines (e.g., submission, first review, and resubmission dates) are accurately tracked to better monitor the permit process timeliness and identify delays and causes for delays; b. ensuring factors such as preliminary review work, new permit versus permit extensions, and Road Allowance Control System versus email submissions are tracked, to enable accurate processing time metrics and help staff identify delay causes; and c. expanding the use of online submissions to reduce reliance on manual email submission and improve efficiency of the permit process. 2.
