Accessibility and Snow Clearing

AdoptedMay 9, 2025 · Toronto City Council

A recommendation that transportation staff attend its June 23, 2025 meeting to explain snow removal plans and their impact on people with disabilities, after slow clearing in February goes to the committee.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

The City of Toronto’s inadequate and excruciatingly slow snow removal process following February's major snowfall had a disproportionate impact on people with disabilities and left our streets and sidewalks impassable for far too long. For people with disabilities, this failure is not just an inconvenience—it is a barrier to mobility, independence, and safety. When sidewalks remain covered in snow and ice for days, wheelchair users, seniors with mobility challenges, and those who rely on assistive devices are effectively trapped in their homes. This issue extends beyond simple delays; it represents a serious failure to provide equitable access to public spaces. Toronto has made commitments to accessibility and inclusivity, yet every winter, the city’s inability to clear snow promptly forces many residents into isolation. What was decided

The Toronto Accessibility Advisory Committee recommends that the Infrastructure and Environment Committee: 1. Request the Manager of Transportation Services and the head of Transportation Road Operations and Maintenance, along with any other relevant departments, to present to the Toronto Accessibility Advisory Committee at its June 23, 2025 meeting, on what they plan to do about inadequate snow removal services and its impact to the disability community.
