Investing In Youth, Empowering Our City’s Potential: Creation of a Toronto Youth Employment Program

Referred back for more workFeb 26, 2025 · Toronto City Council

A proposal for a Toronto Youth Employment Program would ask the federal and provincial governments for funding and direct staff to build a career-focused youth jobs strategy. The federal government proposed $200.5 million for the national Canada Summer Jobs program in 2025-26. The committee sent the item to staff for a report back in late 2025 with an inventory of existing programs and funding needs.

The official summary names a figure of $200.5 million. Council sent it back for more work.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

City Council on December 17 and 18, 2024, referred Motion MM24.2 to the Economic and Community Development Committee for consideration. Youth unemployment in Toronto is at crisis levels, with the city experiencing the highest rates since 2014, with exception made for 2020 and 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic. This lack of economic opportunities disproportionately impacts equity-deserving groups such as Black, Indigenous, and youth living in priority neighbourhoods or Toronto Community Housing. The consequences are severe - youth firearm arrests have spiked 161 percent over the past two years, with youth perpetrating two-thirds of violent carjackings. Research shows clear links between youth unemployment and increased risks of violence, crime, and justice system involvement. What was decided

The Economic and Community Development Committee: 1. Referred the item to the Executive Director, Social Development, Finance and Administration for further consideration, with a request to consult relevant City partners and include in a fourth quarter of 2025 report-back: a. An inventory of existing City of Toronto youth employment programs; b. Opportunities and resources required, including intergovernmental funding, for new or expanded City of Toronto youth employment programs, in order to achieve the target of up to 10,000 additional summer jobs for young people by 2026; and, c. Alignment of youth employment definitions, objectives and targets with City commitments, including but not limited to: Inclusive Economic Development Framework, and Poverty Reduction Strategy and the Year One Progress Report on the Action Plan for Toronto's Economy.
