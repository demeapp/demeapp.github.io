Addressing Dangerous Dogs - Response to 2024.EC16.3

Adopted with changesApr 23, 2025 · Toronto City Council

The animals bylaw would be amended so dangerous dog warning signs can be required on an owner's property and on apartment or condo unit doors, and staff would publish clearer guidance on dangerous dog decisions, if council agrees.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

The City is committed to continuous improvement of dangerous dog investigation processes, prevention of dangerous acts, and enforcement of Dangerous Dog Orders. In November 2024, staff reported to City Council on the recent steps taken to address dangerous dogs, including the standardization of the dangerous dog warning sign, development and launch of the Dangerous Dog Orders map , and a comprehensive public education campaign ( 2024.EC16.3 ). City Council requested additional information from the City Solicitor and Municipal Licensing and Standards as part of 2024.EC16.3 , including information on the number of children bitten by dogs, dangerous dog signage in condos, information on the dangerous dog map, and the quantification of severity. What was decided

City Council on April 23 and 24, 2025, adopted the following: 1. City Council amend Toronto Municipal Code Chapter 349, Animals, to amend section 349-15.1A(4) so it reads: (4) That a warning sign is posted in the form and location required by the Executive Director, with such locations potentially including, but not being limited to, the following: (a) on the owners' private property in a location where it is visible to any person passing or entering the property; (b) on the outside of the unit door for the apartment unit or condominium unit where the dog owner resides. 2.
