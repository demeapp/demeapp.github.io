Feasibility of Pedestrian Priority Phase at Yonge Street and Eglinton Avenue

Referred back for more workSep 26, 2025 · Toronto City Council

Council got a study on a pedestrian scramble at Yonge and Eglinton, with staff recommending against building it even though foot traffic seemed high enough.

Council sent it back for more work.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

This report is prepared in response to a request from the Infrastructure and Environment Committee regarding the feasibility of implementing a Pedestrian Priority Phase (PPP), also known as a ‘Pedestrian Scramble’, at the intersection of Yonge Street and Eglinton Avenue following the commencement of the Eglinton Crosstown Light Rail Transit operations. Transportation Services staff conducted an analysis and reviewed the feasibility of implementing a Pedestrian Priority Phase at this location with the goal of reducing pedestrian delay and eliminating conflicts between pedestrians and turning vehicles, thereby enhancing safety and comfort for vulnerable road users. The investigation determined that a Pedestrian Priority Phase at this location is warranted, as the intersection observed over 5,000 pedestrians per hour in November 2024, making this one of the busiest pedestrian intersections…

What was decided

The Infrastructure and Environment Committee: 1. Referred item IE24.3 back to the General Manager, Transportation Services and requested that they reconsider the Feasibility of a Pedestrian Priority Phase at Yonge Street and Eglinton Avenue, and report to Infrastructure and Environment Committee once the Eglinton LRT is operational through the area of Yonge Street and Eglinton Avenue.
