Advancing Indigenous Affordable Housing - by Councillor Paula Fletcher, seconded by Deputy Mayor Ausma Malik

Real estate staff could negotiate handing 1 to 3 Strathmore Boulevard to the Indigenous housing provider Wigwamen, if council agrees. Metrolinx took Wigwamen's 429 Pape Avenue property for the Ontario Line.

The official record

What the item proposed

Pursuant to 2010.CD32.2, City Council authorized the transfer of twenty of Toronto Community Housing Corporation’s scattered houses to Wigwamen Incorporated to enable solutions to housing issues facing vulnerable persons in a Council designated priority group. On August 1, 2025, Metrolinx expropriated 429 Pape Avenue, one of the scattered housing sites, from Wigwamen Incorporated to advance the construction of the Ontario Line. 429 Pape Avenue had been operated by Wigwamen Incorporated since 2010 as a single-family home. Since the expropriation, the City has been working collaboratively with Wigwamen to identify alternative sites to replace 429 Pape Avenue, with a particular focus in Ward 14. City Council on October 8 and 9, 2025, adopted the following: 1. City Council authorize the Executive Director, Corporate Real Estate Management or their designate, to negotiate and execute a nominal transfer agreement with Wigwamen Incorporated or a related entity for the transfer of 1 to 3 Strathmore Boulevard together with adjoining lands previously expropriated from 17 and 19 Dewhurst Boulevard on such terms and conditions as may be deemed appropriate by the Executive Director, Corporate Real Estate Management, in consultation with the Executive Director, Housing Secretariat and the Director, Indigenous Affairs Office, and in a form acceptable to the City Solicitor. 2.
