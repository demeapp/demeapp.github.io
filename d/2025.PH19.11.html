Toronto City Council · Decision

Signage to Identify Privately Owned Publicly-Accessible Spaces

AdoptedMar 18, 2025 · Toronto City Council

Having staff study whether city signs on city property can point out nearby privately owned public spaces approved before 2014. Those older sites cannot be forced to post signs. Council decides.

A citywide decision.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

I am writing to request your support to have Staff investigate the feasibility of creating signage on City property that highlights Privately Owned Publicly-Accessible Spaces (POPS) that were secured prior to the 2014 POPS design guidelines coming into force. These sites were unfortunately not considered in the initial guidelines requested by myself and former Councillor Adam Vaughan in 2012. Unlike the sites secured after 2014, the City does not have the ability to require property owners to post signage advertising that POPS sites are available for public use. The inability to post signage has created the conditions for exclusionary private uses of some POPS site in Toronto. What was decided

The Planning and Housing Committee: 1. Requested the Chief Planner and Executive Director, City Planning, in consultation with the General Manager, Transportation Services, and the City Solicitor to report to the Planning and Housing Committee in the fourth quarter of 2025 on the feasibility of posting signage on City property to denote adjacent Privately Owned Publicly-Accessible Spaces (POPS) secured prior to the 2014 POPS design guidelines.
