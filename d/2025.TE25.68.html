Toronto City Council · Decision

Elm Avenue Traffic Calming

AdoptedSep 18, 2025 · Toronto City Council

The Toronto and East York Community Council voted on adding three speed humps to Elm Avenue between Mount Pleasant Road and Sherbourne Street North, and on studying longer no-stopping hours near Branksome Hall school.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

A large private school, Branksome Hall, is located at the intersection of Mount Pleasant Road and Elm Avenue. There is heavy traffic in the area, especially at student drop off and pick up times. This traffic has been exacerbated by three construction projects in the vicinity, as well as by a sharp increase in cut through traffic since the pandemic. This heavy traffic causes conflict with the school’s neighbours and reduces the willingness of parents to let their children walk to school, which in turn worsens vehicle traffic. The school retained a traffic consultant which made multiple recommendations, including for traffic calming measures on Elm Avenue and for a traffic light at the intersection of Elm Avenue and Sherbourne Street North. This report was followed by extensive consultations between school, parents, and neighbors, with the assistance of the South Rosedale residence…

What was decided

The Toronto and East York Community Council: 1. Authorized the installation of speed humps on Elm Avenue, between Mount Pleasant Road and Sherbourne Street North. 2. Directed the City Solicitor to prepare a By-law to alter the roadway for the installation of 3 speed humps on Elm Avenue, between Mount Pleasant Road and Sherbourne Street North, generally as shown on Attachment 1 to the letter (September 17, 2025) from Councillor Dianne Saxe. 3. Directed the General Manager, Transportation Services to evaluate expanding no standing/no parking times on Elm Avenue, between Mount Pleasant Road and Sherbourne Street North, to 7:00 a.m. to 9:00 a.m. and 3:00 p.m. to 5:00 p.m., and to report back to the Toronto and East York Community Council by November 2025.
