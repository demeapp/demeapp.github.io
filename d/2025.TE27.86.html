Improving Traffic Calming Measures on Gladstone Avenue, between Bloor Street West and Hallam Street

AdoptedNov 27, 2025 · Toronto City Council

The item proposes three speed humps added on Gladstone Avenue between Bloor Street West and Hallam Street to fill gaps between existing humps, after residents raised speeding worries.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

Earlier this summer, Transportation Services and I met with local residents for a neighbourhood walk in the Bloor Street West and Gladstone Avenue area to discuss local community concerns and solutions. A key issue raised was regarding speeding and dangerous driving on Gladstone Avenue in gaps between existing speed hump locations. In 2000, the approved speed hump locations for Gladstone Avenue created a gap of over 90 metres at some locations. Residents are now experiencing the impact of that decision as speeding drivers race towards each speed hump. I am recommending the installation of additional speed humps on this section of Gladstone Avenue to reduce the dangerous gapping and match current City standards for the spacing of speed humps. What was decided

The Toronto and East York Community Council: 1. Authorized the installation of traffic calming (three additional speed humps) on Gladstone Avenue, between Bloor Street West and Hallam Street, generally as shown Attachments 2 and 3 to the letter (November 24, 2025) from Councillor Alejandra Bravo.
