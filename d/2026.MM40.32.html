Enforcing the Westray Amendments to Keep Workers Safe - by Mayor Olivia Chow, seconded by Councillor Amber Morley

AdoptedApr 22, 2026 · Toronto City Council

Mayor Olivia Chow asks council to push the province and the police board to train and resource enforcement of the Westray criminal-negligence law protecting workers, timed to the April 28 Day of Mourning.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

On May 9, 1992, 26 miners at the Westray Mine in Nova Scotia died in an explosion. Advocacy for better protections for workers and a subsequent Royal Commission led to changes to the law around criminal negligence, which allowed for employers to be held accountable when workers are killed or injured on the job. Police and prosecutors have used this law to prosecute employers who are negligent in protecting their workers, including in Toronto in the Metron case. However, with over 1,000 workers a year still dying on the job across Canada, more needs to be done. This motion makes requests of the Province and the Toronto Police Service Board to ensure that the training and resources are in place to ensure that the Westray Law is being enforced and workers are being protected. Reason for urgency: April 28 is the National Day of Mourning for workers killed or injured in workplace accidents…

What was decided

City Council on April 22 and 23, 2026, adopted the following: 1. City Council request the Province of Ontario to ensure that Crown Attorneys and health and safety regulators are trained in the application of the Westray Amendment, and ensure that there are sufficient resources for training and investigation of workplace fatalities or injuries. 2. City Council request the Toronto Police Service Board to ensure that all police officers have training on how to apply the Westray Amendment and the resources necessary to investigate workplace fatalities and injuries.
