Studying forcing utility firms to compensate customers for outages caused by sloppy cable installation.

Official title: Holding Utility Companies Accountable for Avoidable Service Interruptions - by Councillor Anthony Perruzza, seconded by Councillor Mike Colle

AdoptedJul 29, 2026 · Toronto City Council
No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

Utility infrastructure located within the public right-of-way is essential to ensuring residents have access to telephone, internet, and cable services. However, utility wires are frequently left open to easy inadvertent damage when buried to an insufficient depth, draped over fences, or left on top of grass. Improperly installed utility cables and infrastructure are easily damaged and these incidents can result in prolonged service outages, leaving residents without access to vital communications and internet connectivity for multiple days. Utility providers should be accountable for service interruptions and provide customers with appropriate bill reductions or refunds when outages occur due to shoddy work.

What was decided

City Council on July 29 and 30, 2026, adopted the following: 1. City Council request the General Manager, Transportation Services, to investigate the feasibility of establishing a framework requiring utility providers to provide bill reductions, refunds, or other forms of compensation to customers who experience disruptions to telephone, internet, or cable, services resulting from easy and inadvertent damage to improperly installed utility infrastructure.
