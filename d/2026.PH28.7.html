Toronto City Council · Decision

Advancing Partnerships with the Community Housing Sector to Build More Affordable and Supportive Homes

AdoptedMar 25, 2026 · Toronto City Council

City Council decided whether to let staff advance partnerships with community housing providers to build more affordable and supportive homes. The item cites $21.6 million in federal funding for 54 supportive homes in South Parkdale, $2.6 million a year in provincial operating funding, and up to $8,100,000 in city funding for 54 more supportive homes on hospital-leased land. It also flags a provincial funding request of about $16 million in 2026, growing to $23 million in 2027, for 700-plus homes under construction.

The official summary names a figure of $21.6 million.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

Since the adoption of the HousingTO 2020-2030 Action Plan, the City of Toronto has taken a leadership role in advancing permanent housing solutions across the housing continuum, including supportive housing. Since 2020, over 1,000 new supportive homes have been built (90% of which were City-led), and over 800 more supportive homes are under construction (27% of which are City-led). This growth in supply is the result of advocacy on the part of the City and housing and homelessness service providers, and new partnerships with federal and provincial governments and the health and community sectors. A whole of government and whole of community approach has been successful in restarting growth in the supportive housing system in Toronto, even as the broader housing market faces ongoing challenges from high construction costs, supply chain instability, and trade disruptions.
