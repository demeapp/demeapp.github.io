54 Gormley Avenue - Application to Remove a City Owned Tree

AdoptedJul 8, 2026 · Toronto City Council

The community council decided whether to reopen an earlier decision and allow the removal of a smelly female ginkgo tree at 54 Gormley Avenue.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

As part of the ongoing Tree By-Law Review, City Council has requested that Environment, Climate and Forestry provide recommendations specific to City-owned female gingko trees. The fruit produced by female ginkgo trees emits a noxious smell and toxic residue which inhibits residents from conducting proper maintenance. While staff’s review is underway, this motion allows the residents of 54 Gormley to replace their existing female ginkgo tree with a less hazardous tree. I respectfully request your support for my motion to re-open item TE23.20, delete the previous decision and adopt the recommendations from Environment, Climate and Forestry staff permitting the removal of the tree.

What was decided

The Toronto and East York Community Council: 1. Re-opened Item TE32.20. 2. Deleted its decision on Item TE32.20 and adopted the following recommendation in the report from the Director, Urban Forestry, Environment, Climate and Forestry attached as Attachment 1 to the letter (June 19, 2026) from Councillor Josh Matlow: a. Approve the request for a permit to remove one City-owned tree located at 54 Gormley Avenue as part of an interim approach to addressing female ginkgo trees in the City of Toronto, so that the City-owned female ginkgo tree will be removed and replaced by the City of Toronto in the road allowance fronting 54 Gormley Avenue to the satisfaction of the Executive Director, Environment, Climate and Forestry.
