Audible Stop Announcements on Line 5 and Line 6 - by Commissioner Dianne Saxe, seconded by Chair Jamaal Myers

Adopted with changesApr 16, 2026 · Toronto City Council

The TTC Board decided the TTC's position on a dispute over audible stop announcements on Line 5 and Line 6, where Metrolinx owns the vehicles.

No recorded vote. Made without a name-by-name vote, so no member's position is attached to it in the public record. Only about 9 in every 100 Council decisions has one.

The official record

What the item proposed

In 2005 and 2007, the Human Rights Tribunal of Ontario (Tribunal) ruled that TTC must consistently and reliably announce aloud all subway stops (2005) and bus and streetcar stops (2007). In both cases, the Tribunal made a series of consequential orders, such as appointing an Official Monitor to oversee TTC compliance and to work with the parties to resolve any disputes and requiring TTC to audit its route stop announcements and monthly report its level of compliance to the Tribunal. These orders continue to be in force. Monthly monitoring and reporting has continued for about 20 years, showing little variation and a high level of compliance. There is now a dispute relating to the application of Tribunal’s previous decisions as they relate to Line 5 and Line 6. What was decided

Subject to approval by the Official Monitor as appointed by the Ontario Human Rights Tribunal, the TTC Board approved the following recommendations: 1. That the TTC adopt a quarterly audit cycle for stop announcement compliance requirements arising from Lepofsky v. TTC #1 (2005) and Lepofsky v. TTC #2 (2007), applicable across all TTC operated modes, including Lines 5 (Eglinton Crosstown) and 6 (Finch West), with audit findings consolidated and reported on a quarterly basis to the Tribunal, the Official Monitor, the Ontario Human Rights Commission and David Lepofsky. 2. That the TTC advise the Human Rights Tribunal of Ontario, and the Official Monitor, that it will consent to the application of the audible route stop announcement requirements in Lepofsky v.
