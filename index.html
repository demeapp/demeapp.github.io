Stories over time

Each story groups decisions or recorded divisions whose titles or subject terms share a set of words, and lists them newest first, year by year. Toronto's stories draw on council decisions from January 2018 onward, Ontario's on Assembly divisions from July 2018 onward, and the federal stories on Commons divisions from October 2004 onward — each level's own span, because that is how far back each record in this archive goes. Every story runs to its latest item, shown on its card, and the cards within each legislature are ordered by that date, newest first. A story is a finding aid only — the record itself is unchanged, and an item can appear in more than one story. Not every item can be grouped: a division identified only by a motion number, with no subject in its published title — “Government motion 1”, for example — appears in no story, and a theme exists at a level only where that level's record supports one.

Why Deme exists

Most Toronto decisions leave no names behind

Most Toronto council decisions come with no recorded vote — no public list of who voted yes and who voted no. Deme keeps the whole record readable, and flags every decision where the names are on it, so you can see exactly how your representative voted. At Queen's Park and Parliament Hill the record works the other way: every item Deme lists there is a recorded division, with each member's vote attached. How Deme works →

The gap Deme fills

Built to complete the record, not just cover it

Existing tools do part of the job. Deme picks up where they stop: a plain-language record matched to your ward and your ridings.

  • GovTrackUnited States

    What it does well: tracks US Congress bills and member voting records.

    What's missing: US federal only — nothing for Canadians at any level.

  • openparliament.caCanada (federal)

    What it does well: excellent, searchable Hansard and House of Commons votes.

    What's missing: stops at the federal level — nothing provincial or municipal, and no ward-level view.
