The lobbying record

Under the Lobbying Act, lobbyists report their communications with designated public office holders — members of Parliament among them — to the Office of the Commissioner of Lobbying of Canada. The reports name the client, the lobbyist, the office holder, and the subject. Deme carries that record for the House of Commons: 172,864 communications, gathered onto the profile of every MP they name, free for anyone to read.

Free, on every profile

Each MP's profile carries their lobbying record — how many communications name them, the organizations behind the most of them, the subjects that come up most, and how the contact was made — and the data files behind it are published on the open data page. That is the record, and it stays free.

Coming for subscribers: watching it closely

Reading one profile at a time only goes so far. Deme is building subscriber tools for the people who watch lobbying — journalists, researchers, civic and community groups, and residents who follow the file — so they can see who met whom, and spot the patterns across the whole record. And the record is joined: on every MP's profile, the communications that name them already sit beside their votes, their expenses and their disclosures. See who lobbied them — then see how they voted. The subscriber tools will search and alert across that joined record:

Alerts

Follow the companies, industries, issues and members you watch. When a communication matching them is filed — a meeting arranged with an office holder — you hear about it, instead of finding it months later.

Search across the whole record

One search across all 172,864 filed communications — by the client paying, by subject matter, by the member lobbied, by the lobbyist who filed the report — to see who met whom, and how often, with the member's votes, expenses and disclosures on the same record.

Digests

A regular digest of the newest filings for the names and issues you follow: who is seeking time with whom, on what, in one reading.

Exports

Take any search out as a file — for your reporting, your research, or your group's files.

The record stays free

The subscriber tools change nothing about the record itself. Every MP's lobbying record stays on their profile, free, exactly as it is today, and the data files stay published on the open data page. Subscribers will pay for the tools — the alerts, the cross-record search, the digests, the exports — never for the record, and never for anyone's votes or positions. The record is public. That is the whole idea of Deme.

These tools cover the federal record. Ontario's registry publishes registrations, not communication reports, so there is no Ontario communication record for a tool like this to watch — and Deme does not pretend otherwise. Toronto's record is Council's own, and stays as it is.
