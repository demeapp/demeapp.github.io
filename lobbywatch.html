LobbyWatch

Under the Lobbying Act, lobbyists report their communications with designated public office holders — members of Parliament among them — to the Office of the Commissioner of Lobbying of Canada. The reports name the client, the lobbyist, the office holder, and the subject. Deme already carries that record for the House of Commons: 172,864 communications, gathered onto the profile of every MP they name. LobbyWatch is the tool being built on top of it — for the people who work with this record every day: journalists, researchers, and government-affairs professionals.

What LobbyWatch will do

Alerts

Follow the companies, lobbying firms, issues and members you cover. When a communication matching them is filed, you hear about it — instead of finding it months later.

Search across the whole record

One search across all 172,864 filed communications — by client, by lobbying firm, by subject matter, by the member lobbied — instead of one profile at a time.

Digests

A regular digest of the newest filings for the names and issues you follow: who is lobbying whom, on what, in one reading.

Exports

Take any search out as a file — for your own analysis, your newsroom, or your records.

The record stays free

LobbyWatch changes nothing about the record itself. Every MP's lobbying record stays on their profile, free, exactly as it is today, and the data files stay published on the open data page. Subscribers will pay for the tools — the alerts, the cross-record search, the digests, the exports — never for the record, and never for anyone's votes or positions. The record is public. That is the whole idea of Deme.

LobbyWatch covers the federal record. Ontario's registry publishes registrations, not communication reports, so there is no Ontario communication record for a tool like this to watch — and LobbyWatch does not pretend otherwise. Toronto's record is Council's own, and stays as it is.
