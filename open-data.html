The record, as data

Deme is built from public records. The same data files this website loads are published at public addresses, in the open. No account, no key — the City's data is shared under the City's open licence. Take them, check them, build with them.

Where the record comes from

Everything starts with the City of Toronto's published council records: the agendas, decisions, and voting records the City Clerk posts through TMMIS, the Toronto Meeting Management Information System. Those postings are the City's official proceedings. Every decision page on Deme links its item's official record in that system.

The City publishes members' attendance and voting records itself, two ways: as the Members of Toronto City Council Voting Record dataset on the Toronto Open Data Portal, and as attendance and voting reports in TMMIS. Deme reorganizes the record; it does not originate it. Where Deme and the official record ever differ, the official record is right.

How Deme processes it

Each decision's brief is a plain-language summary prepared from the official record. The extra line some decisions carry — why the decision matters — is composed by template from the record's own fields. If a line can't be sourced from the record, it isn't written.

Every item keeps the name of the body that decided it: City Council, a community council, a committee, or a board. Of the 33,796 decisions in the archive, 13,158 are City Council's own. The Vote page and the Council alerts carry City Council business only — committee and board items never appear there.

Story threads group decisions by documented keyword rules over their titles and subject terms. The grouping is conservative: when in doubt, a decision is left out. The rules themselves ship inside the threads file, so anyone can check exactly how a thread was built.

Reuse it

The underlying records are the City of Toronto's. The City shares its open data under the Open Government Licence – Toronto, and Deme adds no licence and no restrictions of its own to the City's data. Credit the City of Toronto as the source of the record. The briefs are Deme's summaries — check the official record before you rely on one.

Method notes and limits

Votes cast means Yes or No. The City's voting record also carries rows for members who were absent — 112,140 of them — and rows where a member declared a personal interest — 119. Neither is a vote. Of 743,677 vote rows in the record, 631,418 are votes actually cast.

Attendance is a plain statistic. It is a member's votes cast divided by their votes cast plus their absences, across recorded votes. Declared interests are left out of the count entirely: stepping back from a vote for a declared interest is a duty, not an absence, and Deme never counts it as one.

Most decisions have no recorded vote. A name-by-name vote exists only where the City recorded one — for 3,171 of 33,796 decisions, about 1 in 11. For the rest, the public record attaches no member's position, and Deme says so rather than guessing.

The two archives start in different years. The decision archive runs 2018–2026. The voting record reaches back to 2006, five terms. Some long-serving members' totals include votes from before 2018; their item-by-item record starts in 2018.

Freshness. The archive is current to October 19, 2026. The most recent Council meeting in it is July 29, 2026. A new Council term begins in November 2026. Its decisions and votes will be added as the new Council meets and the City publishes its record.

The record belongs to the public. Deme makes it readable — and, with this page, portable.
