Deme is built from public records. Every data file this website loads is published at a public address, listed below — no account, no key; the City's data is shared under the City's open licence. One file is the exception: the bulk dataset — every decision card in a single file — is sent by email on request. Email ourvote.app@gmail.com for it.

Where the record comes from

Everything starts with the City of Toronto's published council records: the agendas, decisions, and voting records the City Clerk posts through TMMIS, the Toronto Meeting Management Information System — the City's official proceedings. Every decision page on Deme links its item's official record in that system.

The City publishes members' voting records itself, two ways: as the Members of Toronto City Council Voting Record dataset on the Toronto Open Data Portal, and as votes and attendance reports in TMMIS. The City publishes no attendance register; Deme's participation figure is derived from the voting record, as the method notes below explain. Deme reorganizes the record; it does not originate it. Where Deme and the official record differ, the official record is right.

How Deme processes it

Each decision's brief is a plain-language summary of the official record — How Deme works explains how the briefs and the why-it-matters line are written.

Every item keeps the name of the body that decided it: City Council, a community council, a committee, or a board. Of the 33,796 decisions in the archive, 13,158 are City Council's own. The Vote page and the Council alerts carry City Council business only — committee and board items never appear there.

Story threads group decisions by documented keyword rules over their titles and subject terms — conservatively: when in doubt, a decision is left out. The rules ship inside the threads file, so anyone can check how a thread was built.

Reuse it

The underlying records are the City of Toronto's. The City shares its open data under the Open Government Licence – Toronto, and Deme adds no licence and no restrictions of its own. Credit the City of Toronto as the source. The briefs are Deme's summaries — check the official record before you rely on one. To reuse a file, take it from its public address above — the bulk dataset is the one exception: email us for it, and we'll send it.

Method notes and limits

Votes cast means Yes or No. The City's voting record also carries rows for members who were absent — 112,140 of them — and rows where a member declared a personal interest — 119. Neither is a vote. Of 743,677 vote rows in the record, 631,418 are votes actually cast. The 119 are a count of recorded votes flagged “interest declared”, not entries in the City's official declarations register; the nature of each interest is not in this data.

Recorded-vote participation is a plain statistic. A member's votes cast divided by their votes cast plus their absences, across recorded votes where a vote was theirs to cast — the share of recorded votes where the member was present and voting. It is not a count of meetings attended; the City of Toronto publishes no attendance register. Declared interests are left out of the count entirely: stepping back from a vote for a declared interest is a duty, not an absence, and Deme never counts it as one.

Most decisions have no recorded vote. A name-by-name vote exists only where the City recorded one — for 3,171 of 33,796 decisions, about 9 in every 100. For the rest, the public record attaches no member's position, and Deme says so rather than guessing.

The two archives start in different years. The decision archive runs 2018–2026. Member totals reach back to 2006, five terms, as aggregates; individual, name-by-name vote rows are stored from 2018 onward only. Some long-serving members' totals include votes from before 2018; their item-by-item record starts in 2018.

Freshness. The archive is current to October 19, 2026; the most recent Council meeting in it is July 29, 2026. A new Council term begins in November 2026; its decisions and votes will be added as the new Council meets and the City publishes its record.
